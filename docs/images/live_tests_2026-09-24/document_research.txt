Here are the sections that describe experimental or system limitations in the paper, organized by page.

Page 5 — Large-scale evaluation (agentification failures)

"Among the 100 computational biology papers, 74 were successfully agentified, yielding 599 proposed tools, of which 593 passed automated validation. The major failure modes among the remaining papers were missing executable code, missing data or model artefacts, environment or dependency failures, and non-generalizable scripts."

Page 7 — Discussion (not every paper can be agentified)

"Not every paper can be seamlessly turned into a robust agent. … a substantial fraction of repositories still could not be successfully agentified, typically owing to incomplete codebases, missing documentation or unresolvable environment configurations."
"We emphasize that open-ended scientific reasoning, including hypothesis generation and mechanistic interpretation, remains human-in-the-loop."
"Paper2Agent can generate hypotheses, propose validation strategies, and execute analyses at scale, but researchers remain responsible for selecting directions and evaluating evidence. We therefore view Paper2Agent as a tool for augmenting scientific discovery … rather than as an autonomous or authoritative source of scientific conclusions."

Page 7–8 — Discussion (benchmark interpretation and analytical validity)

"For open-ended analyses, however, multiple answers may be defensible, so benchmarks based on agreement with a single reference should be interpreted primarily as measures of faithful execution rather than of analytical validity. Evaluating the range of defensible answers will be important for assessing AI systems on open-ended scientific tasks."

Page 8 — Discussion (maintenance and security challenges)

"paper agents require ongoing maintenance as upstream codebases and dependencies evolve; we view this as an inherent feature of publishing executable research rather than a reason to forgo agentification."
"Exposing research code as executable agents also introduces security, intellectual property and attribution challenges that warrant careful handling (Supplementary Note)."

---

Summary of limitation themes (across pages 5, 7, and 8):

Codebase incompleteness: missing code, data, or model artefacts; unresolvable environment/dependency failures; non-generalizable scripts.
Human-in-the-loop required: open-ended scientific reasoning, hypothesis generation, and mechanistic interpretation remain human responsibilities.
Benchmark limitations: open-ended analyses can have multiple defensible answers, so single-reference benchmarks measure faithful execution, not analytical validity.
Maintenance burden: paper agents need ongoing updates as upstream dependencies evolve.
Security, IP, and attribution risks: exposing executable research code as agents introduces novel concerns that require careful handling.
